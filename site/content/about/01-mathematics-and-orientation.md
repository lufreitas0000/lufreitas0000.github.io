I am **Lu Freitas**, a theoretical quantum-matter physicist. I completed my PhD (`2020–2024`), MSc (`2019–2021`), and BSc (`2015–2018`, *cum laude*) in Physics at the Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Norte (UFRN, Natal, Brazil) under the supervision of Prof. Rodrigo G. Pereira, including a Visiting Doctoral Research period (`2022–2023`) at **Heinrich-Heine-Universität Düsseldorf** (Germany) hosted by Prof. Reinhold Egger.

My interest in theoretical physics comes from a love of both physical reality and pure mathematics. Since before entering university, I have felt that mathematics and physics are art forms as well as ways of understanding nature. Attending mathematics courses at IMPA (Instituto de Matemática Pura e Aplicada) and pursuing independent study in algebra, topology, analysis, quantum field theory, and conformal field theory helped cultivate that perspective alongside my training in condensed-matter physics.

A decisive turning point came in 2019, during the first year of my master's degree, when I read Alexei Kitaev's 2006 paper on anyons in an exactly solved honeycomb model. Seeing a concrete lattice spin Hamiltonian lead naturally into Majorana fermions, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge structures, and the algebraic theory of anyonic excitations showed me how structural mathematical reasoning illuminates concrete many-body mechanisms. The books and papers of Hal Tasaki, Xiao-Gang Wen, and Masaki Oshikawa—together with Pedro Vieira's lectures and *Beautiful Papers* approach—have similarly shaped the clarity and physical grounding I value in theoretical work.
