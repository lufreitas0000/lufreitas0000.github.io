A final-year undergraduate course in History and Philosophy of Science in 2018 became my favorite course of the degree. Exploring demarcation, confirmation holism, the interpretation of evidence, and scientific realism sparked a lasting interest in **social epistemology** and **feminist epistemology**.

I am currently reading Helen E. Longino's *Science as Social Knowledge: Values and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry* (Princeton University Press, 1990), which examines how scientific objectivity arises not from isolated individuals free of perspective, but from communities practicing transformative criticism under shared public standards and diverse perspectives. William P. Thurston's essay *On proof and progress in mathematics* similarly informs how I view the interplay between formal proof, numerical computation, and human understanding.
